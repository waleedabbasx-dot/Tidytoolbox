A free photo to text converter built for printed pages

Upload a photo of printed text, a book page, a sign, a typed document, a receipt, a label, and this tool reads it and turns it into editable text. Review the result, fix anything it got wrong, then download it as a PDF or a real Word document, not a screenshot, actual selectable text.

This runs on the same open source text recognition engine used across the web, tuned for printed characters. It reads printed text very well. It can sometimes read neat, well spaced handwriting too, though that is a bonus rather than the main job, cursive or fast handwriting usually will not come through cleanly. Everything happens in your browser, your photo is never uploaded to a server.

Under the hood this is an OCR tool (optical character recognition): it reads the letters in your picture and turns them back into real, editable, searchable text. That covers the everyday jobs an image to text converter gets used for:

  • Copying text from a picture, screenshot, or photo instead of retyping it
  • Converting a photographed page of a book, article, or handout into editable text
  • Turning a scanned document back into a Word file you can actually edit
  • Extracting a quote, address, or reference from a sign, slide, or whiteboard photo

From picture to Word document or PDF

Once the text is extracted you can copy it, download it as a PDF, or export a real Word document (.docx) — not a picture pasted into a page, but genuine text you can edit, restyle, and search. That makes this a free photo to Word converter as well as a plain image to text tool.

Recognition happens entirely in your browser; the photo is never uploaded. Results are best with printed text — books, documents, signs, screenshots — photographed straight-on in decent light. Handwriting recognition is offered as a best effort extra, but neat print will always come out far more accurately.

Is this photo to text converter free?

Yes. Extracting text from a photo and downloading it as a PDF or Word document is completely free, with no account and no watermark.

Does it work on handwriting?

Sometimes, for neat and well spaced handwriting, but printed text is what this tool is built for and reads reliably. Cursive or fast handwriting is a much harder problem for any free tool, always check the extracted text before downloading.

Can I get the result as a Word document, not just a PDF?

Yes. Once the text is extracted, you can download it as either a PDF or a real DOCX file that opens directly in Word or Google Docs.

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. The photo and the text recognition both happen locally in your browser, nothing is sent to a server.

What kind of photos work best?

A clear, well lit, straight on photo of printed text works best: a book page, a printed document, a sign, or a label. Blurry, dark, or heavily angled photos are harder for any text recognition engine to read accurately.

How do I copy text from an image?

Upload the image, let the OCR pass finish, and the recognized text appears ready to copy to your clipboard. You can also download it as a PDF or Word document instead.

Can it convert a photo into a Word document?

Yes. After the text is extracted you can export a real .docx file with editable text, which opens in Microsoft Word, Google Docs, and LibreOffice.

What kind of images work best for OCR?

Clear, well-lit, straight-on photos of printed text give the best results. Blurry photos, strong angles, decorative fonts, and handwriting all reduce accuracy.
